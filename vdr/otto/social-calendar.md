## Day 5 — Monday, March 9

### Morning Post (LinkedIn + X)
**Topic:** A framework for evaluating AI opportunities in your firm
**Copy:**
```
Before you buy any AI tool, run it through this filter:

1. **Is this a repeat task?** AI excels at things humans do hundreds of times. If it happens once a quarter, skip it.

2. **Is the data structured?** Invoices, contracts, emails — yes. Creative strategy meetings — not yet.

3. **What's the error cost?** AI that misroutes an email is annoying. AI that misclassifies a legal document is dangerous. Match the tool to the stakes.

4. **Can you measure the outcome?** "We feel more productive" isn't a metric. "We process 3x more documents in the same time" is.

5. **Who owns the data?** If you can't answer this question clearly, stop and figure it out before anything else.

I've seen firms waste six figures on AI tools that fail at question 1.

Start with the problem. Not the technology.

#AI #ProfessionalServices #Strategy
```

End of day. Quick note on something I keep running into.

When firms first set up AI systems — even well-designed ones — there's always a period where the humans don't trust the output. They double-check everything. They second-guess the model.

This isn't a bug. It's the adoption curve.

The fix isn't better AI. It's:
→ Showing the reasoning (not just the answer)
→ Starting with low-stakes tasks
→ Letting people catch the AI's mistakes (builds confidence)
→ Celebrating when the AI saves time (reinforces the habit)

Technology adoption is a human problem dressed in technical clothing.

The best AI implementation I've ever done wasn't technically impressive. It was just designed around how people actually work.
